### 2.4 How deep is the atomic-orbitals content?  [KEY NEGATIVE FINDING — VERIFIED]
The official syllabus devotes **one clause** to the quantum treatment: "Descrición mecano-cuántica do átomo",
plus "Propiedades periódicas" and "Xeometría e hibridación".

**Explicitly absent from the official guide (I searched the full text of the contents page):**
- the words *quantum numbers / números cuánticos* — NOT present
- *orbitals / orbitales / orbitais* — NOT present
- *electron configuration / configuración electrónica* — NOT present
- *radial distribution function, nodes, nodal planes, orbital shapes* — NOT present
- *molecular orbital theory / orbitales moleculares* — NOT present
- *Schrödinger equation* — NOT present
- **Hybridisation (hibridación) — PRESENT** (topic 2, under "xeometría e hibridación")

So: the guide **names** a quantum-mechanical description of the atom, periodic properties and hybridisation,
but **does not specify** whether orbitals are treated with radial distribution functions, nodes, orbital
shapes, or whether MO theory is taught at all. **I could not verify the actual in-class depth from any
official source** — UVigo publishes no lecture-by-lecture breakdown, and the atomic part is not separated
from the rest of topic 2 (which also covers ionic/metallic bonding and intermolecular forces).

Structural inference (labelled as inference, not an official claim): the 9 ECTS must cover all 10 topics
(stoichiometry, gases, thermodynamics, equilibrium, kinetics, organic, inorganic, industrial chemistry,
biorefinery). Topic 2 is one of ten. The contact-hour plan below gives no separate allocation to atomic
structure. This strongly implies a **descriptive/qualitative** treatment — but this is my reading, not a
documented statement by UVigo.

### 6.2 Misconception categories documented in this literature
Grouped as follows. ⚠️ Attribution caveat: the *studies* are verified, but I could not read their full texts,
so I cannot tie each individual claim to a specific page/quote. Treat the mapping as the field consensus,
not as a quoted finding.

- **Orbital = orbit / Bohr-model persistence.** Students keep planetary/shell mental models and read orbital
  diagrams as trajectories. (Core subject of Taber 2002, Tsaparlis & Papaphotis 2002/2009.)
- **Reification of the "electron cloud".** The boundary-surface diagram is treated as a real surface, a
  container, or a physical object with a fuzzy edge; the cloud is confused with the electron itself.
  ("The Cloud Is Not the Electron"; also the "orbitals shown as surfaces" criticism found in Spanish
  master's-thesis literature at Universidad de Zaragoza: https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/86705/files/TAZ-TFM-2019-644_ANE.pdf)
- **ψ vs ψ².** Confusion between the wavefunction (signed, can have nodes/negative lobes) and the probability
  density |ψ|² (non-negative). Students often think the drawn lobes *are* the wavefunction or that "nodes"
  are places with negative density.
- **Nodes (radial vs angular) and radial distribution functions.** Failure to distinguish radial nodes from
  angular nodal planes; confusion between R(r) (which peaks at the nucleus for ns) and the radial
  distribution function 4πr²R(r)² (which peaks at the most probable radius); believing the most probable
  radius is where R(r) is maximal. (Explicitly part of the Tsaparlis & Papaphotis quantum-concept battery.)
- **Quantum numbers.** m_l misread as "the orbital's orientation in space" without meaning; m_s vs m_l
  conflation; the notion that each electron "owns" an orbital; n as a purely geometric shell radius.
- **Hybridisation (sp³).** Hybrid orbitals treated as physically existing entities rather than a
  mathematical/linear-combination model; belief that electrons are first "promoted" and then hybridised;
  directionality and equivalence of sp³ orbitals misunderstood. **(This is the specifically documented
  focus of Nakiboğlu 2003 and the Algerian studies.)**
- **Molecular orbital theory.** Bonding/antibonding as "attraction/repulsion between atoms", LCAO not
  understood, delocalisation resisted. **(Taber 2002, "Compounding quanta".)**
- **Probability vs determinism; uncertainty principle.** Expectation of definite electron paths.
